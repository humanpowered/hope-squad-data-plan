What the system needs to do

  • Public, live dashboardCurrent-year survey results, updating automatically, filterable by school, district, state, date, grade and length of membership.
  • Built-in pre/postChange on every survey scale, with charts that show it meaningfully.
  • Every nationwide surveyPre/Post, MHRF and Stakeholder end-of-year, each with its own dataset.
  • Locked historyPrior years read-only, with year-over-year by school as a later goal.
  • Chart downloadsViewers can download charts as filtered; downloads of raw data stay off.
  • EmbeddingThe public report can be embedded on hopesquad.com.
  • Matched-pair chartsIdeas such as a histogram of change and a response-transition matrix. Matched-pair charts become possible once responses can be linked, to be scoped in discovery.
  • Organizational KPIsSquads, advisors trained, lessons, renewals, revenue and cost per school from the CRM and finance systems.

User-level permissions

Who can see what

Google's documentation describes "filter by email address" as row-level data security that "is available for any kind of data source," and its data governance guide says it "applies to both the no-cost version of Data Studio and to Data Studio Pro." Google has renamed Looker Studio to Data Studio, so newer pages use that name.

PublicNo sign-inA separate report that only uses totals. Any group of fewer than 10 students is hidden, and individual responses never reach it.
Schools and districtsGoogle sign-in, own data onlyEmail filtering on the restricted data, driven by an access list that maps each email to squad or district IDs. This replaces most raw data requests that now come to support@hopesquad.com.
Hope Squad staffFull internal viewStaff sign in and see everything except information that identifies students.
Viewers must sign in with a Google account to see a filtered report, and must agree to share their email address. This applies even if the report is shared publicly. School staff on Microsoft accounts can create a free Google account using their work email.
What to plan forThe access list must be updated every school year, and email matching is case-sensitive. It sits with Option B's SQL maintainer. Small-number suppression still applies in partner views, because a counsellor may know every student in a squad of 12.
What Looker Studio Pro addsOrganization-owned reports instead of personal or shared-admin ownership, team workspaces with roles, Google Cloud access control, scheduled delivery and alerts, and support. Budgeted as an optional shared line.

For comparison, row-level security in Power BI generally requires a paid licence for every viewer, which is one reason Looker Studio suits a partner-facing dashboard better.

Built in from day one

Student privacy and sensitive data

Hope Squad's own documents point to specific risks: a public dashboard filterable down to small squads; source data holding initials, birth year and birth month; a planned monitoring view of whether any member is suicidal; and respondents in Canada. This is a checklist for counsel familiar with student privacy and does not constitute legal advice.

FERPAStudent-level data from schools is likely an education record, shared under the "school official" exception.
PPRASurveys asking about mental or psychological problems trigger parent notification and opt-out, and written consent in some cases.
COPPAOnline collection of personal information from children under 13 needs parental consent. This applies to the elementary program.
State, district and Canadian rulesState student privacy laws, district data privacy agreements, and provincial law for Canadian squads.
HIPAA-level safeguardsProbably not legally required, but it is the standard parents and funders will assume.
Research ethicsIRB review and data use agreements for anything intended for publication.

Design rules

  1. Public data is aggregated and suppressed. Any result based on fewer than 10 students is hidden, however the filters are combined.
  2. Identifiers stay out of public and partner data sources. Initials, birth dates and research IDs are kept out entirely; hiding them from charts is not enough.
  3. Viewer downloads of underlying data are switched off. Chart image export can stay.
  4. The monitoring view. Before we build the monitoring view, we'll confirm with your team how flagged responses are handled today, so the dashboard supports your existing protocol.
From discovery to maintenance

The process, phase by phase

Seven phases, each ending in a decision gate where leadership can stop, adjust or continue. About five to eight months from kickoff to launch.

  1. 0

    Mobilize

    1–2 WKSCharter, sponsor, core team, decision rights
    • Decide the FormAssembly switch-over timing. Moving at the end of a pre/post cycle is cleanest; otherwise every question and answer must match exactly across both platforms.
    Gate: scope and timing agreed
  2. 1

    Discovery

    3–5 WKSData inventory, survey and KPI catalog, privacy obligations register
    • Interview leadership, programs, research, development, finance and two or three school advisors.
    • Inventory every spreadsheet, survey and system, and collect the questions the board and funders ask most.
    Gate: priorities agreed
  3. 2

    Requirements and design

    2–3 WKSRanked use cases, KPI definitions, first codebook draft
    • Start the codebook, since the FormAssembly forms are built from it.
    • Name the SQL maintainer.
    Gate: Option A or B confirmed
  4. 3

    Proof of concept

    2–3 WKSOne pre/post scale and one KPI dashboard on real, de-identified data
    Gate: approach confirmed
  5. 4

    Build

    10–16 WKSFinal codebook, FormAssembly surveys, loads, data model, access list, CRM and finance data, dashboards
    • Build FormAssembly surveys and the data model in parallel.
    • Align historical survey data to current Squad IDs through a school-name crosswalk.
    • Use the CRM squad list, filtered by state, for the survey's school question, and check the school ID students type against the school they select.
    • Reconcile totals against existing reports before anyone sees a dashboard.
    Gate: data quality sign-off
  6. 5

    Privacy sign-off and launch

    4–6 WKSCounsel review, confirmed handling of flagged responses, training, handover
    • Nothing goes public until counsel has reviewed the checklist and the handling of flagged responses is confirmed.
    • Train the customer experience team to give schools their squad codes and partner sign-in details.
    Gate: staff and partners using it
  7. 6

    Operate and improve

    ONGOINGOperating rhythm
    • Weekly: check that new survey data arrived; follow up on any error alerts.
    • Monthly: data quality checks, small fixes, cloud spend against budget.
    • Each school year: roll over surveys, squad list and access list; archive and lock the prior year.
    • Annually: access, privacy, retention, licence and cost review; roadmap update.
    Gate: annual review
Keeping it healthy

Governance, sized for Hope Squad

Executive sponsorSettles definition disputes and owns the budget.
Data ownersOne each for programs, research, development and finance.
Data steward and SQL maintainerKeeps the codebook, transformations and access list current.
Privacy leadDistrict agreements, consent and incident response.

Core policies: the codebook as the single definition of every question and measure; organization-owned accounts in place of the shared admin login; access tiers with named approvers reviewed annually; small-number suppression on every shared report.

Notes on each line and sources

The $25 minimum for Option B. Every service stays free: BigQuery under its free limits, free Dataform, the Sheets connector, a locked BigQuery dataset instead of PostgreSQL, Fivetran's free plan, and no Pro. Only about $2 a month of storage, logging and secrets remains.

Consultant discount. The donated portion of consultant time may qualify as an in-kind contribution of specialized professional services, which Hope Squad can record and report to its board and funders. Its accountant should confirm the treatment. Option A needs roughly the same consultant effort as B: it skips the Google Cloud setup and the email-filtered dataset but spends more time on formulas and percentage sheets.

FormAssembly. No published prices. Plans are Collect, Automate, Enterprise and Gov Cloud, with API access on all and integrations such as Salesforce added per connection. Response volume probably exceeds the Collect tier's 12,000 a year, and HIPAA-level needs may require a higher plan.

BigQuery. The free tier covers 10 GB of storage and 1 TB of queries a month. The conservative case allows for heavy dashboard traffic at about $6.25 per additional TB. BI Engine is optional acceleration at roughly $30 per GB a month.

PostgreSQL. Supabase Pro or Google Cloud SQL, to keep the research ID key and access list physically separate. A locked BigQuery dataset can do the same for $0, which is the likely case.

Data movement. The FormAssembly Sheets connector is the simplest route. The API route uses Cloud Run and Cloud Scheduler, mostly within free tiers. Fivetran's free plan covers up to 500,000 monthly active rows, which is probably enough for CRM data.

Transformation and semantic layer. Dataform is free inside BigQuery and is the default. Shared metric definitions live in Dataform reporting views and shared Looker Studio data sources at no software cost; maintaining them is part of the SQL maintainer's hours. dbt Cloud Starter is about $100 per user per month and includes a basic semantic layer.

APIs. FormAssembly includes API access on every plan; Google Sheets and BigQuery APIs fall within free quotas at this volume; CRM APIs normally sit within licence limits. The allowance covers overages and AI-assisted matching of historical school names.

Legal review. Hope Squad's existing legal work on its questionnaires, district agreements, PPRA notification and FERPA obligations is assumed to carry over; this will be confirmed in discovery. The budget covers only a targeted review of what this project adds: public school-level reporting, partner sign-in access, new vendors holding student data (FormAssembly and Google Cloud), and research IDs that link pre and post answers. Counsel is assumed at $300 to $400 an hour. The one-time review ($0, $2,000 and $5,000) buys none, about 7 and about 12 hours; the minimum assumes existing counsel signs off within current arrangements. The annual refresh ($0, $500 and $1,200) buys none, about 2 and about 3 hours. Staff time for the annual access review is covered by the maintenance hours.

Excluded. CRM licences (already in place), staff time to write survey questions, and costs to schools.
